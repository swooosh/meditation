# FLOW-02 - Deep Immersion

## Overview
**Duration**: 25 minutes  
**Family**: FLOW (Flow State)  
**Intensity**: Medium  
**Experience Level**: Intermediate

## What This Session Is For
- Primary goal: reduce entry friction and stabilize sustained task immersion.
- Protocol-specific aim: support continuity, timing, and reduced self-monitoring during deep work.
- Best use case: immediately before coding, writing, composing, or skill execution blocks.

## Session Methodology
Think of this as runway time: establish rhythm first, then carry that cadence directly into your work set.

### Phase Map
- Phase 1: **Entry** (0:00-5:00) - Transition from scattered attention into coherent focus.
- Phase 2: **Deepen** (5:00-20:00) - Extend immersion while minimizing self-monitoring.
- Phase 3: **Sustain** (20:00-25:00) - Maintain continuity of the trained state with low effort.

## Mechanism (How It Influences State)
Predictable groove plus reduced cognitive switching cost supports sustained task coupling and reduced self-evaluation.

In practical terms, the protocol combines:
- Repetition for state stabilization
- Gradual transitions to prevent abrupt overactivation
- Cue-based timing to guide attention, breath, and pacing

## Theory (Why This Design)
Based on flow theory: clear temporal structure, manageable challenge, and reduced self-monitoring increase immersion likelihood.

This is a training protocol, not a one-off effect. Benefits usually accumulate with repeated practice.

## What Listeners Typically Notice
- Early response: entry friction drops and task coupling improves.
- Mid-session shift: time sense may compress or stretch during immersion.
- End-state: exit preserves momentum rather than abrupt drop-off.
